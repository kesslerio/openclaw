# Website Design Learnings from YC / Framer CEO Review

**Source:** [Why Your Startup Website Isn't Converting - Y Combinator](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=leQ89XSHILw)
**Featuring:** Jorn Van Dijk (CEO, Framer)
**Date Extracted:** Jan 30, 2026

---

## 🎯 TOP TAKEAWAYS FOR COPPER DIGITAL

### 1. SHOW THE PRODUCT (Highest Leverage)

> "I'm not going to book a demo without getting just a little bit of a taste of what the product looks like and how it works."

**Action for Copper:**

- Add screenshots of the actual iCare dashboard
- Add a short video walkthrough (2 min max)
- Consider: "See it in action" > "Book a demo" as primary CTA

### 2. Be Specific, Not Generic

> "Visually a lot of these illustrations look nice... but they don't communicate information to me and they don't help me understand the product."

**Action for Copper:**

- Replace generic AI illustrations with actual product screenshots
- Use real numbers: "Reduced no-shows by 34%" not "Improve efficiency"
- Show specific use cases for home health (EVV, scheduling, patient calls)

### 3. Clear Value Prop in 5-10 Seconds

> "You really have to make it obvious in 5 to 10 seconds. That's a long time for people to try and figure out if this is for them."

**Action for Copper:**

- Hero should instantly answer: What is this? Who is it for? What does it do?
- Add a sub-headline that literally explains the product
- Don't rely on animations to communicate — tell directly

### 4. Animations: Use Sparingly & Purposefully

> "Animation is pretty challenging to do well... it can be distracting very fast."

**Best use cases for animation:**

- Drawing attention to something important
- Making something overly obvious
- Guiding user to next action (pulsing CTA button)

**Bad animation:**

- Moving too fast
- Too many things animating at once
- Vague metaphors that don't communicate clearly

### 5. Let Users Try Before Signup

> "How can you get somebody to experience the value of the product as quickly as possible? The login/signup is the biggest blocker you could possibly put up."

**Ideas for Copper:**

- Demo voice agent right on homepage (like Leaping AI did)
- Let visitors hear a sample call
- Interactive ROI calculator (we have this!)
- Show results before requiring email

### 6. Social Proof Must Be Specific & Believable

> "No company name, every word is capitalized... it seems sort of made up."

**Requirements for credible testimonials:**

- Full name + company + title
- Specific results with numbers
- Natural writing (not every word capitalized)
- Recognizable logos help

### 7. Typography & Spacing Matter

> "Spacing can be improved... that's the designer curse. A detail like that feels very minor, but it leads to that impression of like something is off here."

**Details that erode trust:**

- Inconsistent spacing
- Text that's too wide (needs max-width)
- Random font choices
- Small errors signal "not detail-oriented"

### 8. CTAs Should Be Obvious & Not Competing

> "Two book a demo buttons... because there's also a lot of orange on the page, the buttons kind of get lost."

**CTA best practices:**

- One clear primary action
- Contrasting color from page
- Consider: "Try it free" > "Book a demo" (lower friction)
- Funnel layout: content narrows toward button

### 9. Match Aesthetic to Audience

> "This is for large call centers... but nothing about the aesthetic really drives that home."

**For Copper (targeting home health agencies):**

- Should feel healthcare/professional, not tech-bro
- Consider showing caregivers, patients, agency staff
- Warm but professional colors
- Trust signals for healthcare (HIPAA, compliance)

### 10. It's OK to Have Multiple Pages

> "It's okay to have multiple pages on a website. You can put the about stuff on a separate page."

**Don't overwhelm one page:**

- Keep homepage focused on core value prop
- Move detailed features to sub-pages
- Pricing can be separate
- FAQ can be separate
