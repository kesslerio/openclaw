---

### Objection Handling

#### Objection 1: "Hyro handles high call volumes better."

**Response:**

> "Absolutely, Hyro is designed for hospital call centers processing thousands of patient calls per day. But home health agencies have a different workflow—you're not managing patient appointments, you're managing caregiver shifts and visit verification. iCare is optimized for that specific workflow. We handle high volume too, but with the home health context built in."

#### Objection 2: "Hyro has multi-channel (voice, chat, SMS)."

**Response:**

> "Multi-channel is valuable for hospitals where patients might prefer different contact methods. But in home health, voice is king—caregivers are driving, patients are elderly and prefer phone calls. We're voice-first by design, which means better quality, not spread thin across channels. If you need SMS reminders, we support that too, but our focus is on the channel that matters most for home health."

#### Objection 3: "Hyro has adaptive AI—more sophisticated conversations."

**Response:**

> "Hyro's adaptive AI is impressive for generic patient interactions. But home health conversations are specialized—caregiver confirmations, schedule changes, visit verification. Our AI is trained specifically on home health workflows, not general healthcare. Think of it like this: would you rather have a Swiss Army knife or a specialized tool? Hyro is the Swiss Army knife. iCare is the specialized tool for home health."

#### Objection 4: "Hyro integrates with our hospital systems."

**Response:**

> "And that's perfect if you're a hospital. But home health agencies don't use Epic or Cerner—you use Axxess, ClearCare, WellSky, Homecare Homebase. Those are the systems iCare integrates with. Hyro may integrate with hospital EMRs, but can they sync with your EVV system? That's what drives your Medicaid billing."

#### Objection 5: "Hyro has more features."

**Response:**

> "More features, or the _right_ features? Hyro has breadth—they handle appointment scheduling, patient triage, billing inquiries, everything a hospital needs. iCare has depth—we're laser-focused on home health operations. EVV integration isn't a feature for us, it's the foundation. Caregiver workflows aren't an add-on, they're the core. You don't need a hundred features; you need the ten features that move the needle for home health."

---

### Closing

**When They're Comparing Options:**

> "Here's the bottom line: Hyro is an excellent product for hospitals managing high-volume patient interactions across multiple channels. But you're not a hospital—you're a home health agency. Your challenges are different: EVV compliance, caregiver shift management, visit verification, home health billing. Those aren't add-ons for us—that's our entire product. We're the vertical solution built for your world, not a horizontal platform you have to configure."

**Call to Action:**

> "Let me show you a 15-minute demo focused specifically on EVV integration and caregiver workflows. You'll see the difference between a multi-channel platform and a purpose-built home health solution. Does [time] work?"

## 🎓 Key Learnings

### 1. Multi-Channel ≠ Better for All Use Cases

Hyro's multi-channel approach (voice + chat + SMS) is powerful for hospitals where patients have varied preferences. But in home health, voice dominates. Being voice-first allows us to be exceptional at the channel that matters most.

### 2. Scalability is Relative

Hyro scales to 10,000+ calls/day. That's impressive. But home health agencies rarely need that scale—even a 500-caregiver agency processes <1,000 calls/day. Our scalability is more than sufficient, and our vertical focus means better quality at the volumes home health actually needs.

### 3. Platform Flexibility is a Double-Edged Sword

Hyro's platform approach is flexible—you can build custom workflows. But flexibility requires resources. Home health agencies don't have dev teams to configure platforms. Turnkey solutions win.

### 4. Enterprise-Only Pricing Excludes Most of the Market

Hyro's enterprise pricing ($50k+/month) works for large health systems. But most home health agencies are small-to-mid size. By serving SMBs, we access a larger market that Hyro ignores.

### 5. "Multi-Channel" is a Feature, Not a Strategy

Home health doesn't need multi-channel as much as hospitals do. Caregivers are driving (voice only). Patients are elderly (prefer phone). SMS reminders are nice-to-have, not must-have. Voice-first is the right strategy for the vertical.
